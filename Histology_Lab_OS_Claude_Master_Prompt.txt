2. PRODUCT DECISION AND RESEARCH FOUNDATION

The initial product is a companion to the existing EHR/LIS. Keep official accession assignment, clinical orders, approved gross reports, and diagnostic reporting under their established systems and authorized workflows. This application can own its operational tasks, discrepancy records, and demonstration events.

First identify what to configure, what to buy/integrate, and what to build. ModMed already advertises pathology gross defaults, configurable stains, reporting/tracking, multiple accession sequences, and slide/cassette label printing in many cases [R1]. We do not know what this lab owns or uses. An inefficient local process can reflect configuration, hardware, training, access, or cross-office coordination.

Published evidence supports investigating the problem:
- A 2016 single-center histology improvement study reported labeling errors declining from 1.03% to 0.28% after a barcode-enabled LIS intervention; further workflow changes brought the rate to 0.22%. Manual slide printing and microtomy were major error sources [R6]. These are historical observations, not a promised effect size for our product.
- A University of Florida study found fewer wrong-tissue-in-block incidents after barcode confirmation was reinforced with a workflow hard stop [R7]. A barcode feature without an effective process is insufficient.
- A biopsy grossing study found voice recognition had negligible effect on the prosector's specimen time, while reducing transcription needs [R8]. Source capture is worth exploring, but do not claim it automatically accelerates grossing or prevents every transcription error.
- Observational work at UPMC documented manual work, communication and organizational burdens and emphasized integration with the pathology information system [R9]. Observe actual work before choosing screens.
- CAP's March 2025 practical handling guide addresses identification, discrepancy handling, traceability, and validation of barcode materials/equipment [R5]. Treat it as guidance to review with laboratory leadership, not an exhaustive current accreditation checklist.

Existing products already address parts of this market. Leica CEREBRO describes identification, on-demand printing, tracking and workflow dashboards [R10]. Milestone MacroPATH describes integrated grossing documentation [R11]. Epredia offers direct slide printing [R12]. These vendor descriptions establish available categories, not independently proven results or confirmed interoperability with our lab.

Our differentiation hypothesis is a usable coordination layer that resolves the particular handoff gaps across dermatology offices and the bench. Test that hypothesis. Do not claim to have invented specimen tracking, speech recognition, or the first AI histology platform.

4. MODEL THE LAB CORRECTLY

Use a persistent relational data model with explicit relationships and state transitions. At minimum distinguish:
- Organization/practice and source-system tenant: the namespace for external identifiers.
- Patient reference: the person and source-scoped identifiers. A name is not a unique key.
- Encounter/collection event: a visit or collection event, with source identifier and timing when available.
- Source order/requisition: what was requested, by whom, from which office, with original identifiers and versions.
- Intake item: a provisional receipt/tracking record. Its identifier must look visibly different from an official accession number.
- Accession/case: the official lab identifier from the authoritative system, including its numbering namespace.
- Specimen/part: a distinct submitted specimen, commonly A/B/etc., with its original designation, site and associated order.
- Container: the actual vessel received and its observed label. Model the local container-to-part relationship explicitly rather than assuming every order equals one bottle.
- Cassette/block: the traceable physical carrier and tissue derivative of a part; a part can yield several blocks. Preserve identity as a cassette becomes a processed block.
- Slide: derived from a block, with its own identifier and ordered preparation/stain/level information.
- Work order: authorized additional work such as a PAS, recut, level or other requested preparation. Link to its requesting person/protocol and exact affected material.
- Clarification, assignment, gross record/version, label/print job, tray/manifest, location, custody event, integration event and audit event.

Keep original source values alongside normalized working values. Store source IDs, source update time, import time, provenance, review state and amendment history. Use UTC storage with America/New_York display and clear dates/time zones.

Patient, order, accession, part, block and slide are different entities. Two orders for the same patient do not prove that they belong to one case. One completed block does not imply a complete case. Keep expected, received, prepared and verified quantities separate.

Derive readiness from required child items and unresolved holds. Track physical progress separately from documentation: grossed, processing, embedded, sectioned, stained and QC-reviewed, as applicable under local SOP. Gross-description completion cannot imply that a block is ready for sectioning or a slide for delivery. Derive eligible downstream work from both physical and documentation states. Model partial completion, late arrivals, additional work, cancellations, corrected records and reopened tasks. Represent unknown data as unknown, not as zero, normal, verified or complete.

Never silently merge patients, combine cases, renumber an issued accession, change a specimen site, replace a part designation, or relabel physical material. A discrepancy after accessioning must retain the original references and use the lab's authorized correction process. In the demo, show a review/cross-reference record; do not invent a live EMA correction endpoint.

5. INTAKE AND ROUTING WORKBENCH

Build a fast bench view with a prominent scan/search field, keyboard flow, persistent current-case context and clear identity information. Use synthetic bottle-label images or typed observations and synthetic orders. Include:
- Courier manifest: expected versus received containers, office, arrival, receiver, missing/extra items and unresolved discrepancies. If no source manifest exists, say expected quantity is unknown.
- Comparison panel: order versus observed container versus requisition, including locally required identifiers, site, laterality, procedure, part and collection details.
- Explicit “staff verified against physical container” action. A barcode or OCR read identifies a record; it does not establish what tissue is inside the bottle.
- Related-order review: detect candidates using source-scoped patient references, encounter, collection details and office; show reasons and conflicts. Preserve two separate legitimate encounters for the same patient. Candidate grouping is not a merge.
- A separate lane for conflicts and missing information so the rest of the bench can continue under local policy. Scope holds to the affected material; do not invent clinical release rules.
- Versioned office-to-pathologist mappings with effective dates, coverage exceptions and explicit review for unknown offices. Show the rule that produced the suggestion. Authorized overrides require a reason.
- Distinct names for ordering clinician, assigned dermatopathologist, accessioner and current task owner.

Demonstrate two accessioners opening the same item: a stale update must be detected, not overwrite a colleague's work. Repeated imports and double clicks must not create duplicate cases, tasks or print jobs.

6. CLARIFICATION THAT ACTUALLY CLOSES THE LOOP

Create specimen-specific clarification tasks for site, laterality, orientation/marking, biopsy method, identity conflict, missing container and possible related orders.

Each task needs the affected order/part, original question, discrepancy evidence, responsible office/contact role, lab owner, timestamps, priority, next follow-up time, answer and resolution reviewer. Model drafted, sent, delivery-unknown/failed/delivered, acknowledged, answered, reviewed and resolved as distinct states where applicable.

In the prototype all notifications are simulated and visibly labeled. Do not contact real offices. A future approved connector can send a generic notification directing an authorized recipient to the secure case view; do not put patient information into ordinary notification text by default.

Use a configurable office contact/coverage directory, working hours, escalation owner, reminders and duplicate suppression. AI may draft a concise clarification message from the actual discrepancy. It must not invent a response or resolve the task because a message was sent.

Document that faster response requires agreement with the submitting offices about ownership and escalation. Additional notifications alone may create more noise.

7. DIGITAL DAILY WORKLIST AND GROSSING HANDOFF

Replace the informational purpose of “the bible” with a shared, filterable daily worklist:
- Group by assigned pathologist with doctor name plus configured color.
- Show accession, part/block counts, stage, last confirmed update, holds, assigned owner, approved requested stains and readiness.
- Provide views for accessioning, grossing, histotechnologists and operations.
- Display PAS as explicit text with the originating order/protocol and affected block/slide. Do not determine PAS eligibility from diagnosis text using an LLM.
- Highlight new, changed and canceled work since the user's last acknowledged version. A printed or downloaded snapshot must show generation time and version.
- Offer a compact print/export fallback for approved downtime and transition workflows. Do not assume paper can be removed immediately.
- A change in a source order must trigger reconciliation of affected work. Do not silently erase work already physically prepared.

Grossing capture should open the exact scanned case/part. Provide structured fields alongside an editable narrative: observed specimen type, piece count, dimensions with explicit units, orientation/ink information, cassette allocation and relevant local template fields.

Use a synthetic dictation transcript to demonstrate extraction. Optionally support microphone input if an approved, technically available provider exists; the demo cannot depend on one. Original text/audio reference and proposed structured values must remain distinguishable. Require confirmation of numbers, units and counts. Do not invent missing values, silently convert ambiguous dimensions, assume a template default was observed, or claim to measure tissue from an ordinary image.

Show draft, needs clarification, operator reviewed, approved for downstream use, and source-system update pending/acknowledged as separate concepts. A completed part can appear in the next work queue immediately when the local workflow permits, while incomplete parts remain visible. Clinical record entry stays in the authoritative system unless a supported integration has been established.

Measure grossing work, transcription work, verification work, batch waiting and integration lag separately. Do not promise that this removes Jorge's overtime without discovering the actual constraint. Direct structured entry inside EMA may be a better initial intervention than a second grossing editor.

8. LABELING, SLIDE ASSEMBLY AND BLOCK RETRIEVAL

Build a print-job simulation linked to authoritative IDs and authorized work. Support planned, queued, submitted, acknowledged, completion-confirmed, failed, unknown-outcome and canceled states, adapted to what the real printer can report. Printing and physical label application/verification are distinct events.

If a printer times out after submission, show an unknown outcome and require reconciliation before retrying. A reprint records the reason, operator, original job and replacement status; it must not create a new specimen. Distinguish reprinting a damaged label from ordering an additional slide. Represent retirement of an old physical label through the approved process.

Generate actual synthetic label previews with unique machine-readable identifiers and readable accession/part/block/slide/stain text where appropriate. Validate demonstration encoding/decoding. Do not claim scanner, solvent, processor or stainer validation from a screen preview.

Explain the hardware dependency: direct cassette/slide printing requires compatible printers, drivers or supported interfaces, validated materials, and bench procedures. A web page cannot physically apply 300 stickers. Inspect the lab's existing printer capabilities before recommending new equipment.

For tray assembly, establish a tray and case context once, then verify individual required slides and highlight missing, unexpected, duplicate or canceled material. Do not mark all 20 slides verified from one tray scan. Batch verification can be a future hardware-supported, validated capability, not a shortcut in the prototype.

For block retrieval, model cabinet/drawer/box/slot or another actual addressing scheme; last confirmed location; scan time; current checkout holder; requested destination; returned state; and missing-material investigation. Group a pick list by physical location while preserving the receiving bench's required work order.

Scanning a location does not prove every item in it is present. A last-seen location is not continuous real-time tracking. Support overdue returns, partial retrieval, outside send-outs and conflicting location records. Archive conversion requires an initial inventory and ongoing location discipline.

9. WHERE AI ADDS VALUE

Use explicit rules and database constraints for identity relationships, routing, counts, permissions, accession references, readiness, print deduplication and deadlines. Use AI selectively for:
- Extracting candidate fields from synthetic requisitions or transcripts, with source text/image evidence and unresolved fields.
- Drafting clarification requests for human review.
- Summarizing a case's operational history with links to the underlying events.
- Summarizing pending handoffs and observed bottlenecks from computed data.

If assistants are presented as agents, define their inputs, allowed tools, outputs, trigger, owner, timeout, retry behavior, review requirement and audit events. Prefer a few bounded assistants over many autonomous agents modifying records.

Core workflows must work when the model is unavailable. Use deterministic, clearly labeled demonstration outputs unless a real model is configured. Do not present hardcoded outputs as live reasoning or fabricate calibrated confidence percentages. Treat imported documents as data rather than executable instructions, and keep external content unable to change tool permissions.

Any runtime AI action proposing a change must expose its evidence and remain a proposal until the appropriate review. There is no autonomous diagnosis, stain ordering, specimen matching decision, accession correction or final clinical report release in this prototype.

13. ACCEPTANCE CHECKS THAT MATTER

Implement and run meaningful tests for these behaviors, using the simplest appropriate test level:
1) An unknown office creates a routing exception rather than a guessed doctor assignment.
2) Candidate A/B related orders preserve both source IDs and require review.
3) The same patient with two different encounters remains separate; identical MRNs from different source practices do not collide.
4) A patient/site mismatch preserves conflicting evidence and blocks the affected transition until authorized resolution.
5) A discrepancy discovered after accessioning cannot silently renumber or merge cases or invalidate printed identities.
6) A grossing draft with an uncertain decimal/unit or missing required piece count cannot be finalized without review.
7) Completing one part updates eligible work while unfinished parts remain incomplete.
8) A printer timeout after submission remains unknown until reconciled; retries and double clicks do not create uncontrolled duplicate jobs.
9) A tray expecting ten slides but containing nine expected and one unexpected slide cannot become verified complete.
10) Scanning a wrong block for a retrieval request shows a mismatch. An old location is displayed as last confirmed, not guaranteed present.
11) Reimporting the same source data is idempotent. An older update cannot silently overwrite a newer reviewed version.
12) Concurrent edits detect conflict. Server-side role checks prevent unauthorized resolution or approval; a demo role selector is not claimed as production authentication.
13) A source outage visibly marks data stale. Restored events reconcile without duplicating work. No source writes are implied by local task completion.
14) A canceled or amended stain order remains traceable and triggers review of work already printed/prepared.
15) AI failure leaves the manual operational workflow usable. Untrusted source text cannot issue tool instructions or alter roles.

Run the critical browser journey, build/type checks and data-model tests. Report exactly what was tested and any gaps. Do not add a large suite of implementation-mirroring tests while leaving the actual scenario untested.

15. ADDITIONAL IMPROVEMENT HYPOTHESES AND DISCOVERY

Investigate these secondary opportunities before committing to more modules:
- Source-office order preparation: structured part/site capture, expected-container manifests and a pre-pickup completeness check. Avoid creating a second order-entry form when the existing EHR can provide these functions.
- Missing transport or fixation timestamps: preserve observed and documented times separately and flag missing data. Do not invent collection/fixation times or clinical acceptability thresholds.

- Office feedback: measure recurring incomplete orders and clarification categories with volume denominators, then improve training/templates collaboratively.
- Outside consultations and archival returns: record material leaving the lab, recipient, expected return and reconciled receipt.

These are proposed discovery areas, not claims that all of these problems occur at our lab. Keep them out of the first build unless the core workflow is complete.
